# OmniQuant: Omnidirectionally Calibrated Quantization for LLMs

arXiv 2308.13137v3 (2023-08, ICLR 2024). Source:
`/srv/hipfire/references/Quant/2308.13137v3-OmniQuant`.

## Verdict for hipfire

**The objective is the lesson. The headline gains mostly evaporate at our
group size.**

OmniQuant replaces hand-crafted quantization parameters with ones optimized by
gradient descent against **block-wise output error**. That is exactly the lever
`next-phase-goals.md` concluded we need, in a section written before this paper
was read:

> RECONSTRUCTION ERROR IS NOT A USABLE OBJECTIVE HERE. It failed to predict
> output quality six times in one session, in both directions ... Any
> calibration work needs the KLD harness in the loop, not an RMSE target.

Two of our open problems are the same problem stated twice:

* **Clip search.** We measured that the MSE search "already picks c = 1.0 (no
  clipping) whenever clipping does not pay", concluded clipping is not a
  per-layer lever, and saw a forced no-clip override make KLD worse
  (0.048006 vs 0.046167). OmniQuant's **LWC** learns the clipping threshold
  against block output error instead of MSE. Our search is not finding nothing
  because there is nothing — it is finding nothing because it is optimizing the
  metric we already proved does not track output quality.
* **Auto mixed precision.** `mixed_precision.rs` ranks tensors by
  `oq4_sensitivity`, imatrix-weighted *reconstruction* error, and underperforms
  hand-picking. Same root cause, same fix direction.

**But check the size of the prize before building it.** OmniQuant's advantage
over AWQ collapses once grouping is used, and we quantize at g256:

    LLaMA-1 7B        RTN    GPTQ    AWQ   OmniQuant   vs AWQ
    W4A16 (per-ch)   6.43    6.13   6.08     5.86      -3.6%
    W4A16 g128       5.96    5.85   5.81     5.77      -0.7%

Grouping already captures most of what learnable clipping does. At g256 and
4 bits, expect the smaller number. The paper's large wins are at W2A16 and W4A4,
neither of which is our target.

## What it actually does

* **LWC** (Learnable Weight Clipping) — optimize the clipping threshold per
  quantization group rather than hand-crafting or MSE-searching it.
* **LET** (Learnable Equivalent Transformation) — channel-wise scale/shift that
  moves quantization difficulty from activations to weights. This is AWQ /
  SmoothQuant made learnable; our `--awq-alpha 0.45` is a single scalar found by
  sweeping KLD by hand, which is the degenerate case of what LET learns
  per-channel.
* Differentiable, block-wise. 7B-70B in 1-16 h on one A100-40G with 128 samples,
  so the cost is PTQ-shaped, not training-shaped.

## What to take from it

1. **Adopt the objective, not necessarily the method.** The single highest-value
   change is making our calibration knobs optimize block OUTPUT error instead of
   reconstruction. That applies to clip search, to `oq4_sensitivity`, and to the
   AWQ alpha we currently hand-sweep.
2. **LET generalizes our alpha sweep.** We found alpha 0.45 optimal by measuring
   KLD across arms — a one-dimensional search over a per-channel object. If
   per-channel scales were fitted against block output error, the sweep would be
   unnecessary and the result should be at least as good by construction.
3. **Temper expectations at g256/4-bit.** 0.7% against AWQ is the honest
   expectation for our configuration. Worth doing for the objective fix, which
   also unblocks auto mixed precision; not worth doing for the headline number.
